Under the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160), the Provincial Assessor appraises and assesses real property (land, buildings, machinery and other improvements) for real property tax purposes, keeps the assessment records and tax maps, and issues certified copies of assessment records on request.

The Provincial Assessor also has technical supervision over the city and municipal assessors and works with them on tax mapping and other assessment work. In the 20 municipalities, most transactions start at the municipal assessor's office and are reviewed and approved at the Provincial Assessor's Office.

Malaybalay City and Valencia City have their own city assessors. If your property is in one of the two cities, go to that city's assessor's office.

## Tax Declaration Services

Declaring new property, new buildings and changes to existing property

A tax declaration shows the market value and assessed value of a property and is the basis for computing real property tax. It is not a land title. Declare new buildings and improvements, and update the record when a property is subdivided, consolidated, reclassified or demolished.

<Steps>

1. **File the request**: Go to the municipal assessor's office of the town where the property is located and submit the documents listed below.
2. **Inspection, if needed**: For new buildings, improvements or reclassification, the assessor's staff may inspect and measure the property.
3. **Appraisal and assessment**: The property is valued using the approved schedule of market values and the assessment levels set by ordinance.
4. **Review and approval**: The assessment is reviewed and approved at the Provincial Assessor's Office, and the tax declaration is released to you.

</Steps>

### Required Documents

<Requirements>

- Proof of ownership, such as the title or deed
- Existing tax declaration, if any
- Building permit and plans, for new buildings
- Approved survey or subdivision plan, if the lot changed
- Valid government-issued ID

</Requirements>

## Transfer of Tax Declaration

After a sale, donation or inheritance of land or a building

<Steps>

1. **Settle national taxes**: Pay the taxes due to the BIR (for example capital gains, donor's or estate tax and documentary stamp tax) and get the electronic Certificate Authorizing Registration (eCAR).
2. **Pay the transfer tax**: For property in the 20 municipalities, pay the real property transfer tax at the Provincial Treasurer's Office. City property: pay at the city treasurer.
3. **Register the transfer**: For titled land, register the deed at the Registry of Deeds to get the new title in the new owner's name.
4. **Apply for the new tax declaration**: Submit the documents to the municipal assessor (or the city assessor for city property). The new tax declaration is issued in the new owner's name after review.

</Steps>

### Required Documents

<Requirements>

- Notarized deed of sale, donation or extrajudicial settlement
- BIR eCAR
- Transfer tax official receipt
- Proof that real property tax is paid up to date
- Certified copy of the new title, for titled land
- Valid government-issued ID

</Requirements>

## Certifications

Various certifications related to real property records

<Requirements>

- Certified true copy of a tax declaration
- Certified copy of the field appraisal and assessment sheet (FAAS)
- Certification of property holdings or of no property
- Tax map or sketch of the lot

</Requirements>

<Steps>

1. **Make the request**: Give the tax declaration number or the owner's name and the property's location, and state the purpose of the request.
2. **Pay the fee**: Pay the certification fee at the treasurer's office and keep the official receipt.
3. **Receive the document**: Show the receipt and your ID to claim the certified copy or certification.

</Steps>

Some records may be released only to the owner, an heir, or a representative with written authorization.

## Assessment Appeals

If you disagree with the assessed value

Ask the assessor's office first to explain how your property was valued, and point out any error in the record, such as the wrong area, classification or owner.

If you still disagree, the Local Government Code lets the owner appeal the assessment to the Local Board of Assessment Appeals of the province (or of the city, for city property), and then to the Central Board of Assessment Appeals. Appeals have deadlines that run from the date you receive the notice of assessment, so act early.

An appeal does not stop the collection of the real property tax. Ask the treasurer's office about paying under protest.
