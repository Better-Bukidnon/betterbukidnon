<Topic id="assessment" theme="blue" icon="house-check" title="About the Office" subtitle="What the Provincial Assessor does under the Local Government Code" prose>

<TopicFacts>

Under the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160), the Provincial Assessor appraises and assesses real property (land, buildings, machinery and other improvements) for real property tax purposes, keeps the assessment records and tax maps, and issues certified copies of assessment records on request.

The Provincial Assessor also has technical supervision over the city and municipal assessors and works with them on tax mapping and other assessment work. In the 20 municipalities, most transactions start at the municipal assessor's office and are reviewed and approved at the Provincial Assessor's Office.

Malaybalay City and Valencia City have their own city assessors. If your property is in one of the two cities, go to that city's assessor's office.

</Topic>

<Topic id="declaration" theme="green" icon="file-text" title="Tax Declaration Services" subtitle="Declaring new property, new buildings and changes to existing property">

<Lead>
  A tax declaration shows the market value and assessed value of a property and is the basis for
  computing real property tax. It is not a land title. Declare new buildings and improvements, and
  update the record when a property is subdivided, consolidated, reclassified or demolished.
</Lead>

<Steps>
  <Step title="File the request">
    Go to the municipal assessor's office of the town where the property is located and submit the
    documents listed below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspection, if needed">
    For new buildings, improvements or reclassification, the assessor's staff may inspect and
    measure the property.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Appraisal and assessment">
    The property is valued using the approved schedule of market values and the assessment levels
    set by ordinance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and approval">
    The assessment is reviewed and approved at the Provincial Assessor's Office, and the tax
    declaration is released to you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Checklist title="Required Documents">

- Proof of ownership, such as the title or deed
- Existing tax declaration, if any
- Building permit and plans, for new buildings
- Approved survey or subdivision plan, if the lot changed
- Valid government-issued ID

</Checklist>

<Note>[See the full guide to tax declarations](/service-details/property-declaration)</Note>

</Topic>

<Topic id="transfer" theme="violet" icon="arrow-left-right" title="Transfer of Tax Declaration" subtitle="After a sale, donation or inheritance of land or a building">

<Steps>
  <Step title="Settle national taxes">
    Pay the taxes due to the BIR (for example capital gains, donor's or estate tax and documentary
    stamp tax) and get the electronic Certificate Authorizing Registration (eCAR).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay the transfer tax">
    For property in the 20 municipalities, pay the real property transfer tax at the Provincial
    Treasurer's Office. City property: pay at the city treasurer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register the transfer">
    For titled land, register the deed at the Registry of Deeds to get the new title in the new
    owner's name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply for the new tax declaration">
    Submit the documents to the municipal assessor (or the city assessor for city property). The new
    tax declaration is issued in the new owner's name after review.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Checklist title="Required Documents">

- Notarized deed of sale, donation or extrajudicial settlement
- BIR eCAR
- Transfer tax official receipt
- Proof that real property tax is paid up to date
- Certified copy of the new title, for titled land
- Valid government-issued ID

</Checklist>

</Topic>

<Topic id="certification" theme="amber" icon="award" title="Certifications" subtitle="Various certifications related to real property records">

<Checklist icon="file-earmark-check">

- Certified true copy of a tax declaration
- Certified copy of the field appraisal and assessment sheet (FAAS)
- Certification of property holdings or of no property
- Tax map or sketch of the lot

</Checklist>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make the request">
    Give the tax declaration number or the owner's name and the property's location, and state the
    purpose of the request.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay the fee">
    Pay the certification fee at the treasurer's office and keep the official receipt.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive the document">
    Show the receipt and your ID to claim the certified copy or certification.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Fineprint>
  Some records may be released only to the owner, an heir, or a representative with written
  authorization.
</Fineprint>

</Topic>

<Topic id="reclassification" theme="cyan" icon="chat-square-text" title="Assessment Appeals" subtitle="If you disagree with the assessed value" prose>

Ask the assessor's office first to explain how your property was valued, and point out any error in the record, such as the wrong area, classification or owner.

If you still disagree, the Local Government Code lets the owner appeal the assessment to the Local Board of Assessment Appeals of the province (or of the city, for city property), and then to the Central Board of Assessment Appeals. Appeals have deadlines that run from the date you receive the notice of assessment, so act early.

An appeal does not stop the collection of the real property tax. Ask the treasurer's office about paying under protest.

</Topic>
