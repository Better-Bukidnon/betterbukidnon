<Notice icon="info-circle" title="Who handles death certificates">
  Deaths are registered with the Local Civil Registry Office (LCRO) of the city or municipality
  where the death occurred: Malaybalay City, Valencia City, or one of the 20 municipalities of
  Bukidnon. This may not be the town where the person lived. The Provincial Government does not
  register deaths or issue death certificates. Requirements and fees vary by city and municipality,
  so confirm with the office before you go. [Find your city or municipality](/government#lgus)
</Notice>

</Section>

<Section alt icon="list-ol" title="Registering a Death" intro="General steps. Your civil registrar may ask for more.">

<ProcessCards>
  <ProcessCard title="Get the Certificate of Death">
    The attending physician fills out and signs the medical part of the Certificate of Death. In a
    hospital, the hospital usually prepares it.
  </ProcessCard>
  <ProcessCard title="No Attending Physician?">
    If the person died at home without a doctor, the city or municipal health officer examines the
    case and certifies the cause of death.
  </ProcessCard>
  <ProcessCard title="Informant Signs">
    A family member or other informant checks the personal details, signs, and presents a valid ID.
  </ProcessCard>
  <ProcessCard title="Submit Within 30 Days">
    File the Certificate of Death with the civil registrar of the city or municipality where the
    death occurred, within 30 days.
  </ProcessCard>
  <ProcessCard title="Get the Burial Permit">
    The city or municipality issues the burial permit, and a transfer permit if the remains will be
    moved to another town. Funeral homes often help with this.
  </ProcessCard>
  <ProcessCard title="Get Your Copies" final>
    The civil registrar can issue a local copy once the death is registered. The record is then sent
    to the PSA, so a PSA copy is available only after some time.
  </ProcessCard>
</ProcessCards>

</Section>

<Section icon="files" title="Two Ways to Get a Copy" intro="Choose the office copy or the PSA copy, depending on what the requesting agency needs">

<RouteGrid>

<ListBox icon="building" title="From the City or Municipal Civil Registrar">

1. Go to the civil registrar of the city or municipality where the death was registered.
2. Fill out the request form: full name of the deceased and the date and place of death.
3. Present a valid ID. A representative also brings an authorization letter.
4. Pay the fee set by the LGU and claim the certified copy.

</ListBox>

<ListBox icon="globe" title="From the PSA">

1. Order online through PSA Serbilis for delivery, or visit a PSA Civil Registry System outlet.
2. Give the full name of the deceased and the date and place of death.
3. Present a valid ID if you go in person, and pay the PSA fee.
4. Receive the copy printed on PSA security paper.

<ListNote>[PSA Serbilis (online requests)](https://www.psaserbilis.com.ph/)</ListNote>

</ListBox>

</RouteGrid>

<ListBox icon="file-earmark-text" title="For Registration Within 30 Days">

- Certificate of Death signed by the attending physician or the city or municipal health officer
- Valid ID of the informant
- Other documents the civil registrar asks for, depending on the case

</ListBox>

<ListBox icon="clock-history" title="For Delayed Registration (After 30 Days)">

- Affidavit explaining the reason for the delay
- PSA negative certification
- Supporting documents, such as a burial or cemetery certificate

</ListBox>

<SectionHeading icon="question-circle" title="Frequently Asked Questions" />

<Faq question="The person died in a hospital in another town. Where do we register?">
  At the civil registrar of the city or municipality where the death occurred, which is where the
  hospital is, not where the person lived.
</Faq>

<Faq question="Does the Provincial Capitol issue death certificates?">
  No. Death records are kept by the civil registrar of the city or municipality where the death was
  registered, and by the PSA.
</Faq>

<Faq question="How much does it cost?">
  Each city and municipality sets its own fees for registration, burial permits and local copies,
  and the PSA sets its own fees. Ask the office for the current amounts.
</Faq>

<Faq question="The PSA has no copy yet. What should we do?">
  A newly registered record takes time to reach the PSA. Ask the civil registrar where the death was
  registered to confirm that the record was sent, or to endorse it to the PSA.
</Faq>

<Faq question="How do I correct an error on a death certificate?">
  Clerical or typographical errors can be corrected by petition at the civil registrar under
  Republic Act 9048. Other changes need a court order.
</Faq>

</InfoMain>
