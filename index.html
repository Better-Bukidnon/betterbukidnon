Brief History of Bukidnon

Key milestones from pre-colonial indigenous heritage to modern provincial governance

Before 1850
Indigenous Homeland

The central Mindanao highlands serve as the ancestral domain of the seven indigenous tribes of Bukidnon.

1850s
Spanish Colonial Integration

Settlements in the plateau are organized under Misamis Province, with Malaybalay established as a pueblo in 1877.

1907
Sub-Province of Agusan

Act No. 1693 establishes Agusan Province with Bukidnon as a sub-province and Malaybalay as its capital.

Mar 10, 1917
Regular Province Status

Act No. 2711 recognizes Bukidnon as a regular province on March 10, 1917—now celebrated as the province's founding anniversary.

1977
First Kaamulan Festival

The provincial government launches the first organized Kaamulan Festival in Malaybalay to honor the customs of the seven tribes.

1998–2001
Component Cities Chartered

Malaybalay becomes a component city in 1998 (RA 8490), followed by the trade hub of Valencia in 2001 (RA 8985).

2012–Present
Four Congressional Districts

Republic Act No. 10184 reapportions Bukidnon into four legislative districts covering 2 component cities, 20 municipalities, and 464 barangays.

A Gathering of Seven Tribes — The Kaamulan Festival

Cultural Heritage

Kaamulan comes from the Binukid word amul ("to gather"). Held annually in Malaybalay City around the March 10 founding anniversary, it unites the seven hill tribes of Bukidnon. Select a tribe below to explore:

Bukidnon (Higaonon-Bukidnon) Northern & Central Plateau (Malaybalay, Impasug-ong, Sumilao, Manolo Fortich)

Meaning 'people of the mountains,' the Bukidnon tribe is renowned for rich oral epics (Olaging), intricate red-white-black panika attire, and traditional datuship governance.

Highlands & Food Basket of Mindanao

Geography & Economy

Anchored by towering mountain ranges and a cool plateau across 10,498.59 km², Bukidnon drives Mindanao's agriculture and watersheds. Select a feature to inspect:

Mount Dulang-dulang · 2,938 m 2nd Highest Peak in the Philippines

Rising 2,938 meters above sea level in the Kitanglad Range, Mt. Dulang-dulang (Katanglad) is a sacred ancestral domain of the Talaandig, Bukidnon, and Higaonon tribes and home to the Philippine Eagle.
